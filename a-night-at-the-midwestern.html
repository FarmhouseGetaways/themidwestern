One night only

A Night at The Midwestern

The regular room was never enough for this one. A whole second space went up in the yard just for the night — string lights, blacklight, and a lounge that didn’t exist the week before or the week after.

Built for the night

An outdoor lounge that only ever existed once

The patio got rebuilt as its own space for the party — hanging light curtains, blacklight-reactive decor, and seating that had never been out there before and went back to storage after.

The lounge

Curtains of string lights under blacklight, glow-in-the-dark accents on the posts and railings, and low seating spread across the deck.

The drink station

A row of glowing beakers and flasks, each one a different color, tubed together like something out of a lab — as much a decoration as a bar.

The photo booth

A pair of blacklight-reactive posters as a backdrop, lit for portraits all night — everyone who stood in front of them came out looking like a different person.

Mr. Robot VR

A VR experience running the whole night, tucked in alongside everything else — one more reason nobody wanted to leave.

The lounge

Every corner of the yard was lit for it

Blacklight and string lights turned the ordinary patio into somewhere else entirely — palm trees glowing at the edges, a glow-in-the-dark trail along the deck, and a lounge table lit up under a haze of light for anyone who wanted to sit down.

Outside

Nobody stayed in one room all night

The crowd moved between the bar inside and the lounge outside all night — a drink at the bar, a portrait at the booth, a turn on the VR rig, then back out to the lounge to do it again.

The photo booth

Stand in front of the posters, come out looking different

Two blacklight-reactive prints on the wall did all the work — anyone who stood in front of them under that light came out looking like a totally different photograph.

More from the night

The rest of the party

A one-time thing

The lounge came down after that night — it was built for the party, not for keeps. This page is what remains of it, alongside everything else that made The Midwestern what it was.
